# New Jersey's rental lead paint inspection law

Reviewed by Lead Paint Roster staff

Last updated 2026-10-31. Checked against the official sources listed at the end of this page. General information, not legal advice. Rules change, so confirm details with the agency named.

New Jersey law P.L.2021, c.182 requires most rental homes built before 1978 to be inspected for lead-based paint hazards. The first inspection was due by July 22, 2024, or at an earlier tenant turnover. After that, inspections are due every 3 years or at turnover, whichever comes first, unless the unit has a valid lead-safe certificate.

At a glance

* Law: P.L.2021, c.182, effective July 22, 2022. Rules: N.J.A.C. 5:28A, adopted May 1, 2023 ([NJ DCA](https://www.nj.gov/dca/codes/resources/leadpaint.shtml)).
* Covers single-family, two-family and multiple dwelling rentals built before 1978, with some exemptions.
* Inspect at tenant turnover and at least every 3 years.
* A passing inspection earns a lead-safe certificate, valid 2 years.
* Fees: a municipal inspection fee plus $20 per unit for the state Lead Hazard Control Assistance Fund ([N.J.A.C. 5:28A-2.2](https://www.nj.gov/dca/codes/codreg/pdf_regs/njac_5_28A.pdf)).
* Penalty after a 30-day cure period: up to $1,000 per week ([N.J.A.C. 5:28A-4.1](https://www.nj.gov/dca/codes/codreg/pdf_regs/njac_5_28A.pdf)).

## Which rentals are covered?

The law applies to single-family, two-family and multiple dwelling rental units built before 1978 ([NJ DCA](https://www.nj.gov/dca/codes/resources/leadpaint.shtml)). These are exempt ([N.J.A.C. 5:28A-1.3](https://www.nj.gov/dca/codes/codreg/pdf_regs/njac_5_28A.pdf)):

* Dwellings built in 1978 or later.
* Single-family and two-family seasonal rentals rented for less than 6 months a year, with no consecutive lease renewals.
* Dwellings certified free of lead-based paint under N.J.A.C. 5:17 (see [lead-safe vs lead-free](https://leadpaintroster.com/nj-lead-safe-vs-lead-free-certificate/)).
* Multiple dwellings registered with the state for 10 years or more with no outstanding lead-based paint violations from their cyclical inspections.
* Units that already have a valid lead-safe certification.

## When are inspections due?

The first inspection was due within 2 years of July 22, 2022 (so by July 22, 2024), or at tenant turnover if that came first ([NJ DCA](https://www.nj.gov/dca/codes/resources/leadpaint.shtml)). After that, inspect every 3 years or at tenant turnover, whichever is earlier. You do not need a turnover inspection while a lead-safe certificate is still valid ([N.J.A.C. 5:28A-2.1](https://www.nj.gov/dca/codes/codreg/pdf_regs/njac_5_28A.pdf)).

DCA's guide gives this example: a unit inspected on July 1, 2024 has a certificate that expires July 1, 2026. If the tenant moves out on October 1, 2026, a new inspection is needed before the next tenant, and the next one is due by October 1, 2029 ([DCA guide](https://www.nj.gov/dca/codes/publications/pdf_lead/lead_based_paint_guide_rental_dwellings.pdf)). A turnover inspection resets the 3-year clock.

## Who performs the inspection, and how?

The municipality's lead inspector (its own staff or a contracted lead evaluation firm) does the inspection. Owners may also hire a DCA-certified lead evaluation contractor directly ([NJ DCA](https://www.nj.gov/dca/codes/resources/leadpaint.shtml)). DCA certifies lead evaluation and abatement firms, and the NJ Department of Health licenses the individual workers ([DCA guide](https://www.nj.gov/dca/codes/publications/pdf_lead/lead_based_paint_guide_rental_dwellings.pdf)).

The method depends on your town ([NJ DCA](https://www.nj.gov/dca/codes/resources/leadpaint.shtml)):

* **Visual inspection** where fewer than 3% of tested children age 6 and under have a blood lead level of 5 µg/dL or higher.
* **Dust wipe sampling** where at least 3% do.

DCA publishes which method applies in each municipality. Check the list on the [DCA lead-safe certification page](https://www.nj.gov/dca/codes/resources/leadpaint.shtml), or ask your local construction or health office. Our [inspection vs risk assessment guide](https://leadpaintroster.com/lead-inspection-vs-risk-assessment/) explains the difference between these services.

## What does it cost?

There is no statewide price. The municipality sets an inspection fee sufficient to cover its costs, plus an additional $20 per unit that goes to the Lead Hazard Control Assistance Fund, unless DCA has already collected it. In common interest communities like condos, the unit owner pays ([N.J.A.C. 5:28A-2.2](https://www.nj.gov/dca/codes/codreg/pdf_regs/njac_5_28A.pdf)). Prices from private firms vary. See our [lead inspection cost guide](https://leadpaintroster.com/lead-inspection-cost/).

## What happens if hazards are found?

The owner must fix them, choosing either abatement or interim controls done to HUD Guidelines ([N.J.A.C. 5:28A-2.5](https://www.nj.gov/dca/codes/codreg/pdf_regs/njac_5_28A.pdf)):

* **Interim controls** (for example paint stabilization or specialized cleaning) are followed by a dust wipe re-inspection. Once the unit passes, it gets a lead-safe certificate valid for 2 years.
* **Abatement** permanently removes the hazard. When abatement ends with a lead abatement clearance certificate, the lead-free certification issued at final clearance exempts the unit from future periodic inspections.

If hazards are found in one unit of a 2- or 3-unit building, the other units must be inspected too ([N.J.A.C. 5:28A](https://www.nj.gov/dca/codes/codreg/pdf_regs/njac_5_28A.pdf)). Interim controls that are not renovation work need no special certification. Paid renovation work in pre-1978 homes still follows EPA's RRP rule, and abatement needs a DCA-certified abatement firm ([DCA guide](https://www.nj.gov/dca/codes/publications/pdf_lead/lead_based_paint_guide_rental_dwellings.pdf)). See [RRP vs abatement](https://leadpaintroster.com/rrp-vs-lead-abatement/).

## Owner paperwork and record keeping

Under N.J.A.C. 5:28A-3.1 ([N.J.A.C. 5:28A](https://www.nj.gov/dca/codes/codreg/pdf_regs/njac_5_28A.pdf)), owners must:

* Give the tenant and the municipality evidence of the lead-safe certificate at turnover, and attach it to the lease.
* Tell the municipality about tenant turnovers.
* Give a buyer a copy of the rules, any certifications and DCA's guidance document when selling a covered rental.

DCA publishes the [lead-safe certification form](https://www.nj.gov/dca/codes/forms/pdf_lead/lead_safe_cert_fill.pdf) inspectors use.

## Penalties

An owner who fails to inspect, remediate or certify gets 30 days to fix it. After that, the penalty is up to $1,000 per week until the violation is fixed. Remediation counts as started once a contractor has been hired ([N.J.A.C. 5:28A-4.1](https://www.nj.gov/dca/codes/codreg/pdf_regs/njac_5_28A.pdf)).

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need a new lead inspection every time a tenant moves out?**

Yes, unless the unit already has a valid lead-safe certificate. N.J.A.C. 5:28A-2.1 says no turnover inspection is needed while a certificate is still valid. Certificates last 2 years.

**How long is a New Jersey lead-safe certificate good for?**

Two years from the inspection that found no lead-based paint hazards. Even with a valid certificate, the 3-year inspection cycle and turnover rules still apply once it expires.

**Is my condo or single-family rental covered?**

Yes, if it was built before 1978 and is not exempt. The law covers single-family, two-family and multiple dwelling rentals. In common interest communities the unit owner pays the fee.

**What is the penalty for not inspecting?**

After a 30-day period to fix the problem, up to $1,000 per week until it is fixed (N.J.A.C. 5:28A-4.1).

**Who do I contact with questions?**

Your municipality's construction or health office, or DCA at leadlaw21pl182@dca.nj.gov (listed on DCA's lead-safe certification page).
