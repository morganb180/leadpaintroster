# NYC Local Law 1: lead paint rules for owners and tenants

Reviewed by Lead Paint Roster staff

Last updated 2026-10-31. Checked against the official sources listed at the end of this page. General information, not legal advice. Rules change, so confirm details with the agency named.

Local Law 1 of 2004 requires owners of New York City buildings built before 1960 (or 1960 to 1978 if the owner knows there is lead paint) to presume the paint contains lead and to find and fix lead hazards, especially in apartments where a child under 6 lives. It adds annual notices, one-time XRF testing, turnover repairs and safe work rules.

At a glance

* Covers multiple dwellings (3+ units) built before 1960, or 1960 to 1978 if the owner knows of lead paint, plus tenant-occupied units in pre-1960 1- and 2-family homes ([HPD](https://www.nyc.gov/site/hpd/services-and-information/lead-based-paint.page)).
* A child "resides" in a unit if they routinely spend 10 or more hours a week there.
* Annual Notice: deliver January 1 to 16, collect by February 15 ([HPD](https://www.nyc.gov/site/hpd/services-and-information/annual-notice-and-investigations.page)).
* One-time XRF paint testing by an independent EPA-certified inspector or risk assessor was due August 9, 2025 ([HPD](https://www.nyc.gov/site/hpd/services-and-information/paint-testing-requirement-exemptions.page)).
* Missing the testing deadline can bring a Class C violation and civil penalties of $1,500 per unit or common area.
* Keep records for at least 10 years.

## Which buildings does Local Law 1 cover?

Per HPD ([NYC HPD](https://www.nyc.gov/site/hpd/services-and-information/lead-based-paint.page)), the law covers:

* Multiple dwellings (3 or more units) built before 1960.
* Multiple dwellings built between January 1, 1960 and January 1, 1978, if the owner knows there is lead-based paint.
* Tenant-occupied units in private dwellings (1 to 2 units) built before 1960.
* Co-op and condo units not occupied by the owner or the owner's family.

In these buildings, owners must presume paint is lead-based unless it has tested negative by an EPA-certified inspector and HPD has granted an exemption. NYC defines lead-based paint as 0.5 mg/cm² or more of lead, a threshold lowered on December 1, 2021 ([HPD](https://www.nyc.gov/site/hpd/services-and-information/paint-testing-requirement-exemptions.page)).

## What is a lead-based paint hazard under NYC law?

HPD says a hazard exists when there is peeling or disturbed paint in a pre-1960 building in an apartment where a child under 6 resides, unless the surfaces have tested below 0.5 mg/cm². Hazards include paint dust (including from doors and windows that rub) and surfaces children have chewed ([HPD](https://www.nyc.gov/site/hpd/services-and-information/lead-based-paint.page)).

## Annual notice and yearly inspections

Every year, owners must ([HPD](https://www.nyc.gov/site/hpd/services-and-information/annual-notice-and-investigations.page)):

1. Deliver an Annual Notice to each tenant between January 1 and January 16, asking whether a child under 6 resides in the unit. HPD provides approved forms.
2. Collect the completed notices by February 15.
3. If a tenant does not respond, inspect the unit at reasonable times to find out. If attempts to get access between February 16 and March 1 fail, notify the NYC Department of Health in writing.
4. Visually inspect every unit where a child under 6 resides at least once a year, and fix deteriorated paint promptly with a properly certified contractor.

Tenants should return the notice, tell the landlord when a child under 6 moves in, and report peeling paint or leaks. Tenants can file a complaint online or call 311 if peeling paint is not fixed or dust is not contained ([HPD](https://www.nyc.gov/site/hpd/services-and-information/lead-based-paint.page)).

## XRF testing requirement

As of August 9, 2025, owners of covered buildings must have all painted surfaces in units and common areas tested once with an XRF device, at the 0.5 mg/cm² level. The tester must be an EPA-certified inspector or risk assessor who is independent of the owner and of any remediation firm ([HPD](https://www.nyc.gov/site/hpd/services-and-information/paint-testing-requirement-exemptions.page)).

* Only one round of testing is required, and vacant units must be tested too.
* Units occupied by the owner or the owner's family do not need testing while they stay owner-occupied.
* Keep testing records, inspector certifications and affidavits for 10 years. HPD offers sample affidavit forms, including one for units where access was refused.
* Failure to comply may result in a Class "C" immediately hazardous violation and civil penalties of $1,500 per unit or common area.

If testing finds no lead-based paint, owners can apply for a Lead-Free Exemption, which ends the annual notice and investigation duties for those units ([HPD](https://www.nyc.gov/site/hpd/services-and-information/paint-testing-requirement-exemptions.page)). Exemptions granted under the old 1.0 mg/cm² standard are revoked at turnover.

## What owners must do at turnover

Before a new tenant moves in, whether or not they have children, owners must ([HPD](https://www.nyc.gov/site/hpd/services-and-information/lead-based-paint-correction-child-under-6-unit.page)):

* Fix all peeling paint and underlying defects such as leaks.
* Remove lead paint from chewed surfaces, or encapsulate them with an approved hard encapsulant.
* Abate door and window friction surfaces, even if the paint is intact, unless they were previously abated. This can mean replacing doors and frames.
* Make bare floors, windowsills and window wells smooth and cleanable.
* Certify the work on the Lease/Commencement of Occupancy Notice, and give the tenant HPD's Lead Paint Hazards in the Home pamphlet.

## Units where a child under 6 lives

In units where a child under 6 resides, owners must do similar abatement-level work by the earliest of: the next turnover; July 1, 2027, for a child residing as of January 1, 2025; or within 3 years after a child begins residing there ([HPD](https://www.nyc.gov/site/hpd/services-and-information/lead-based-paint-correction-child-under-6-unit.page)). This applies whether or not peeling paint is visible. When work cannot be done safely with tenants at home, owners must offer temporary relocation to a suitable lead-safe unit.

## Safe work practices

In pre-1978 buildings, HPD advises following the strictest of the federal and city rules: use lead-safe work practices for any job disturbing 2 square feet or more of paint in a room, 10% or more of a small component, window removal, or 20 square feet or more outside ([HPD](https://www.nyc.gov/site/hpd/services-and-information/safe-work-practices.page)). Work done because of violations or city orders needs an EPA-certified lead abatement firm. After the work, dust sampling must be done by an EPA-certified inspector or risk assessor independent of the owner and contractor. Read more in [RRP vs abatement](https://leadpaintroster.com/rrp-vs-lead-abatement/) and [clearance testing](https://leadpaintroster.com/lead-clearance-testing/).

Penalties for not using safe work practices range from $200 to $2,000 from the Health Department and from $1,250 to $25,000 from the Department of Buildings ([HPD](https://www.nyc.gov/site/hpd/services-and-information/safe-work-practices.page)).

## Help and funding

HPD's Lead Hazard Reduction and Healthy Homes Primary Prevention Program offers federally funded grants for eligible pre-1960 buildings in all five boroughs with low- and very low-income households and a child under 6 or a pregnant woman. Grants average about $20,000 per apartment ([HPD](https://www.nyc.gov/site/hpd/services-and-information/lead-hazard-reduction-and-healthy-homes-program.page)). See [assistance programs](https://leadpaintroster.com/lead-paint-assistance-programs/). HPD's Lead Unit is at (212) 863-5501, option 5 ([HPD](https://www.nyc.gov/site/hpd/services-and-information/annual-notice-and-investigations.page)).

## Frequently asked questions

**Does Local Law 1 apply if no children live in my building?**

Yes, partly. The XRF testing requirement applies to covered buildings whether or not a child under 6 lives there, and turnover work is required for every new tenancy. Annual visual inspections are triggered by a child under 6 residing in the unit.

**When is the Annual Notice due?**

Owners deliver it between January 1 and January 16 and collect it by February 15. HPD's tenant guidance says to return it by February 16, so tenants should return it as early as possible.

**Who can do the XRF test?**

An EPA-certified lead inspector or risk assessor who is not the owner, the owner's agent, or a contractor involved in remediating the building.

**What is the penalty for missing the XRF testing deadline?**

HPD says failure to comply may result in a Class C immediately hazardous violation and civil penalties of $1,500 per unit or common area.

**Can I do lead paint repairs myself?**

Work that disturbs lead paint must follow safe work practices, and work from violations or city orders requires an EPA-certified abatement firm. Dust clearance testing must be done by an independent certified inspector or risk assessor.
