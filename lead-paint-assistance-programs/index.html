Lead paint assistance programs: grants, loans and free testing

Last updated 2026-10-31. Checked against the official sources listed at the end of this page. Program funding, income limits and openings change often, so confirm with the program before you apply.

Reviewed by Lead Paint Roster staff

Most help for lead paint comes from state and city programs funded partly by HUD. New Jersey's DCA runs the Lead Remediation and Abatement Program for lower-income households, New York City's HPD offers grants for pre-1960 buildings, and Massachusetts offers a deleading tax credit and Get the Lead Out loans. Each has its own income and property rules.

At a glance

  • NJ: DCA Lead Remediation and Abatement Program (LRAP), for households at or below 80% of area median income in pre-1978 homes with 1 to 10 units (NJ DCA).
  • NYC: HPD Primary Prevention Program grants, averaging about $20,000 per apartment, for eligible pre-1960 buildings (NYC HPD).
  • MA: tax credit up to $3,000 per unit, Get the Lead Out loans up to $30,000 to $45,000 by home size (Mass.gov).
  • Many cities run HUD-funded lead hazard control programs (HUD).
  • Programs usually pay certified contractors directly rather than paying owners or tenants.

New Jersey

Lead Remediation and Abatement Program (LRAP). Run by the Department of Community Affairs and funded by a $180 million federal grant, LRAP pays for removing lead hazards for low- to moderate-income households (NJ DCA). Eligibility per DCA:

  • Household income at or below 80% of the area median income for your county.
  • You live in a 1- to 10-unit residential property built before 1978. DCA says at least 20% of applications are rejected because the building was built later, so check your deed, original title or your town's records first.
  • Tenants can apply for a state-funded lead inspection, and for remediation or abatement if hazards are found. Landlords can apply on behalf of eligible tenants.

Landlords and tenants do not receive LRAP money directly. DCA funds grantee organizations that manage the work, and contractors must be EPA RRP-certified firms, with DCA certification for abatement. Apply through DCA's online portal, listed on the Lead Assistance Programs page, which also lists the lead agency for each county. DCA Housing and Community Resources: 609-292-4080.

Rental owners facing inspections under NJ's rental lead law should note that the $20 per unit inspection surcharge goes to the state Lead Hazard Control Assistance Fund (N.J.A.C. 5:28A-2.2).

New York City

Lead Hazard Reduction and Healthy Homes Primary Prevention Program (PPP). HPD offers federally funded grants to owners of buildings built before 1960, from small homes to large apartment buildings, in all five boroughs (NYC HPD). Eligibility:

  • The building has lead-based paint that is not intact, confirmed by the program's risk assessment.
  • It is occupied by low- and very low-income households.
  • At least one unit houses a child under 6 or a pregnant woman, or is visited regularly by young children.

Grants average about $20,000 per apartment. In return, rental owners agree for 5 years to rent new vacancies to low- and very low-income tenants, with priority for families with young children. Owner-occupants agree to keep living there for 5 years. Apply by email to hpdlead@hpd.nyc.gov with the application and your recent annual lead notice records. See NYC Local Law 1 for owner duties.

New York State (outside NYC)

The NYS Department of Health's Lead Rental Registry page points owners to New York State Homes and Community Renewal (HCR) funding and HUD lead hazard control grant projects for repairs (NYS DOH). Your local health department can tell you which programs serve your area.

Massachusetts

Massachusetts lists these options (Mass.gov):

  • Lead paint tax credit: the lesser of cost or $3,000 per unit with a Letter of Full Deleading Compliance; up to $1,000 with a Letter of Interim Control, which counts toward the $3,000 (Mass. DOR).
  • Get the Lead Out: low-cost loans for 1- to 4-family homes, up to $30,000 (single-family), $35,000 (2-family), $40,000 (3-family) or $45,000 (4-family). Income-eligible owner-occupants can get 0% deferred loans due at sale, transfer or refinancing. Nonprofits can get 0% and investors 3% amortizing loans for units rented to income-eligible households, over 5 to 20 years. Apply through agencies listed by MassHousing.
  • HUD-funded city programs in Boston, Brockton, Haverhill, Lawrence, Lynn, Malden, New Bedford, Quincy and Worcester.
  • Home Improvement Loan Program (HILP): $7,500 to $50,000 over 5 to 15 years for owner-occupied 1- to 4-family homes and condos.
  • HUD 203(k): roll deleading into a purchase or refinance mortgage for 1- to 4-family homes, with at least $5,000 in repairs.
  • Local programs: CDBG and HOME funds through your city or town's community development office.

See the Massachusetts Lead Law guide for compliance steps.

Federal and other help

HUD's Office of Lead Hazard Control and Healthy Homes funds lead hazard control grant programs and supports the grantees that run them (HUD). Ask your city or county housing or health department whether a HUD-funded program serves your area. For questions about lead in your home, EPA's National Lead Information Center is 1-800-424-5323 (EPA).

Frequently asked questions

Are there free lead inspections in New Jersey?

Eligible tenants can apply to DCA's LRAP for a state-funded lead inspection. Eligibility is based on household income (at or below 80% of area median income) and living in a pre-1978 building with 1 to 10 units.

Can a landlord get a lead abatement grant?

Often yes, if tenants are income-eligible. In NJ, landlords can apply to LRAP on behalf of eligible tenants. In NYC, owners of eligible pre-1960 buildings can apply to HPD's Primary Prevention Program and agree to 5-year rental terms.

Do I get the money directly?

Usually not. LRAP funds grantee organizations that hire certified contractors, and HPD oversees PPP work done by EPA-certified abatement contractors.

Is there a lead paint tax credit?

Massachusetts offers one: up to $3,000 per unit for full deleading compliance and up to $1,000 for interim control.
