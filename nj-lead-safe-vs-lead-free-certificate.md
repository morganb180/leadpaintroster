# New Jersey lead-safe vs lead-free certificate

Reviewed by Lead Paint Roster staff

Last updated 2026-10-31. Checked against the official sources listed at the end of this page. General information, not legal advice. Rules change, so confirm details with the agency named.

In New Jersey, a lead-safe certificate means an inspection found no lead-based paint hazards right now. It lasts 2 years. A lead-free certification means the unit has no lead-based paint at all, or has been abated. It exempts the unit from future periodic inspections under the rental lead law.

At a glance

* Lead-safe: no outstanding lead-based paint hazards. Does not mean lead-free ([N.J.A.C. 5:28A](https://www.nj.gov/dca/codes/codreg/pdf_regs/njac_5_28A.pdf)).
* Lead-safe certificate is valid 2 years.
* Lead-free: no lead-based paint on any surface, or abated, certified under N.J.A.C. 5:17 ([N.J.A.C. 5:17](https://www.nj.gov/dca/codes/codreg/pdf_regs/njac_5_17.pdf)).
* Lead-free units are exempt from periodic rental inspections.
* A lead-safe certificate becomes invalid if a later independent inspection finds a hazard ([DCA guide](https://www.nj.gov/dca/codes/publications/pdf_lead/lead_based_paint_guide_rental_dwellings.pdf)).

## Side-by-side comparison

|  | Lead-safe certificate | Lead-free certification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What it means | No lead-based paint hazards found at inspection. Lead paint may still be present | No lead-based paint on any surface, or the dwelling has been abated |
| Rule | N.J.A.C. 5:28A (rental lead law) | N.J.A.C. 5:17 |
| How you get it | Pass a periodic visual or dust wipe inspection, or a re-inspection after interim controls | Full paint inspection of all painted surfaces, or final clearance after abatement |
| How long it lasts | 2 years | Exempts the unit from periodic rental inspections |
| Issued by | The municipal inspector or a DCA-certified lead evaluation contractor | A certified lead evaluation firm, on a DCA form |

## What a lead-safe certificate means

A lead-safe certification is issued after a periodic inspection finds no lead-based paint hazards. "Lead safe" means there are no outstanding hazards. It does not mean the unit is lead free ([N.J.A.C. 5:28A](https://www.nj.gov/dca/codes/codreg/pdf_regs/njac_5_28A.pdf)). The certificate is valid for 2 years, and no turnover inspection is needed while it is valid. DCA publishes the [certification form](https://www.nj.gov/dca/codes/forms/pdf_lead/lead_safe_cert_fill.pdf).

A certificate becomes invalid if an independent inspection later finds a lead-based paint hazard ([DCA guide](https://www.nj.gov/dca/codes/publications/pdf_lead/lead_based_paint_guide_rental_dwellings.pdf)). Because paint can deteriorate, keep painted surfaces in good condition between inspections.

## What a lead-free certification means

Under N.J.A.C. 5:17, "lead-free" means there is no lead-based paint on any surface in the unit, its common areas or the building. The rule also defines a "lead-free interior" ([N.J.A.C. 5:17](https://www.nj.gov/dca/codes/codreg/pdf_regs/njac_5_17.pdf)). A certified lead evaluation firm issues lead-free certification on a DCA form after inspecting all painted surfaces with XRF, paint chip sampling or another permitted method, following the HUD Guidelines.

A unit can also become lead-free through abatement: when abatement ends with a lead abatement clearance certificate from the construction official, the lead-free certification issued at final clearance exempts the unit from future periodic inspections ([N.J.A.C. 5:28A-2.5](https://www.nj.gov/dca/codes/codreg/pdf_regs/njac_5_28A.pdf)).

N.J.A.C. 5:17 also allows a certification after a risk assessment finds the property free of lead-based paint hazards, issued with a caution about ongoing maintenance. That is not the same as lead-free.

## Which should a landlord aim for?

* **Lead-safe** is the minimum for covered rentals. Expect a new inspection at least every 3 years or at turnover once the certificate expires (see [NJ's rental lead law](https://leadpaintroster.com/nj-lead-paint-inspection-law/)).
* **Lead-free** takes more up-front cost (a full paint inspection, or abatement) but removes the unit from the periodic inspection cycle.

A full paint inspection is a different service from a hazard check. See [inspection vs risk assessment](https://leadpaintroster.com/lead-inspection-vs-risk-assessment/), [RRP vs abatement](https://leadpaintroster.com/rrp-vs-lead-abatement/) and [clearance testing](https://leadpaintroster.com/lead-clearance-testing/). ## Paperwork to keep and share

At tenant turnover, give the tenant and the municipality evidence of the certificate and attach it to the lease. When you sell a covered rental, give the buyer the rules, certifications and DCA's guide ([N.J.A.C. 5:28A-3.1](https://www.nj.gov/dca/codes/codreg/pdf_regs/njac_5_28A.pdf)).

## Frequently asked questions

**Is a lead-safe certificate the same as lead-free?**

No. Lead-safe means no lead-based paint hazards were found at inspection. Lead paint may still be present. Lead-free means there is no lead-based paint, or the unit has been abated.

**How long is a NJ lead-safe certificate valid?**

Two years. While it is valid, no turnover inspection is needed.

**Does a lead-free certificate expire?**

N.J.A.C. 5:28A says a lead-free certification issued at final clearance after abatement exempts the unit from future periodic inspections. Units certified lead-free under N.J.A.C. 5:17 are exempt from the rental inspection rule.

**Who can issue a lead-free certification?**

A certified lead evaluation firm, on a DCA form, after inspecting all painted surfaces.
