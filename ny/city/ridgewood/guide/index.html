What should I ask a lead inspector in Ridgewood before hiring?

  • Are you independent of the abatement contractor?
  • What is your firm certification number, and who will do the inspection (their individual credential)?
  • Will you do a full inspection (surface by surface), a risk assessment, or both?
  • Will you use an XRF analyzer, paint chip samples, or dust wipes?
  • Will the report meet the requirements of the local rental-inspection or lead law?

What are New York's lead paint rules?

New York State has no lead-based paint certification of its own, so EPA certifies the firms and individuals who inspect, assess or abate lead paint in pre-1978 housing there. In New York City, Local Law 1 of 2004 adds duties for landlords of older multiple dwellings where young children live.

  • New York State does not run its own lead-based paint activities certification. EPA certifies firms and individuals there, and EPA's locator is the official lookup.
  • In New York City, Local Law 1 of 2004 places lead-paint duties on landlords of older multiple dwellings where young children live. NYC HPD explains the current inspection and XRF-testing requirements.
  • Lead-based paint inspections and risk assessments in pre-1978 housing must be done by EPA-certified inspectors or risk assessors working for an EPA-certified firm.

Sources: EPA lead-based paint program, NYC HPD lead-based paint. Plain-language summary, not legal advice. Confirm with the agency.

How do I verify a lead inspector's certification in Ridgewood?

Look the firm up in the official EPA list, then ask for the firm's certification number and the individual inspector's own credential in writing. Our copy of the list was retrieved 2026-10-12.

  1. Check the firm or person in the official list (US Environmental Protection Agency). Ours was retrieved 2026-10-12, so confirm the certification is still current.
  2. Ask for the certification or licence number in writing, plus the individual inspector's own credential.
  3. Decide what you need first: an inspection or a risk assessment.
  4. For abatement, use a certified abatement contractor, and have someone independent do the clearance test.
