New Jersey lead-safe vs lead-free certificate

Last updated 2026-10-31. Checked against the official sources listed at the end of this page. General information, not legal advice. Rules change, so confirm details with the agency named.

Reviewed by Lead Paint Roster staff

In New Jersey, a lead-safe certificate means an inspection found no lead-based paint hazards right now. It lasts 2 years. A lead-free certification means the unit has no lead-based paint at all, or has been abated. It exempts the unit from future periodic inspections under the rental lead law.

At a glance

  • Lead-safe: no outstanding lead-based paint hazards. Does not mean lead-free (N.J.A.C. 5:28A).
  • Lead-safe certificate is valid 2 years.
  • Lead-free: no lead-based paint on any surface, or abated, certified under N.J.A.C. 5:17 (N.J.A.C. 5:17).
  • Lead-free units are exempt from periodic rental inspections.
  • A lead-safe certificate becomes invalid if a later independent inspection finds a hazard (DCA guide).

What a lead-safe certificate means

A lead-safe certification is issued after a periodic inspection finds no lead-based paint hazards. "Lead safe" means there are no outstanding hazards. It does not mean the unit is lead free (N.J.A.C. 5:28A). The certificate is valid for 2 years, and no turnover inspection is needed while it is valid. DCA publishes the certification form.

A certificate becomes invalid if an independent inspection later finds a lead-based paint hazard (DCA guide). Because paint can deteriorate, keep painted surfaces in good condition between inspections.

What a lead-free certification means

Under N.J.A.C. 5:17, "lead-free" means there is no lead-based paint on any surface in the unit, its common areas or the building. The rule also defines a "lead-free interior" (N.J.A.C. 5:17). A certified lead evaluation firm issues lead-free certification on a DCA form after inspecting all painted surfaces with XRF, paint chip sampling or another permitted method, following the HUD Guidelines.

A unit can also become lead-free through abatement: when abatement ends with a lead abatement clearance certificate from the construction official, the lead-free certification issued at final clearance exempts the unit from future periodic inspections (N.J.A.C. 5:28A-2.5).

N.J.A.C. 5:17 also allows a certification after a risk assessment finds the property free of lead-based paint hazards, issued with a caution about ongoing maintenance. That is not the same as lead-free.

Which should a landlord aim for?

  • Lead-safe is the minimum for covered rentals. Expect a new inspection at least every 3 years or at turnover once the certificate expires (see NJ's rental lead law).
  • Lead-free takes more up-front cost (a full paint inspection, or abatement) but removes the unit from the periodic inspection cycle.

A full paint inspection is a different service from a hazard check. See inspection vs risk assessment, RRP vs abatement and clearance testing. Help paying is listed in assistance programs.

Paperwork to keep and share

At tenant turnover, give the tenant and the municipality evidence of the certificate and attach it to the lease. When you sell a covered rental, give the buyer the rules, certifications and DCA's guide (N.J.A.C. 5:28A-3.1).

Frequently asked questions

Is a lead-safe certificate the same as lead-free?

No. Lead-safe means no lead-based paint hazards were found at inspection. Lead paint may still be present. Lead-free means there is no lead-based paint, or the unit has been abated.

How long is a NJ lead-safe certificate valid?

Two years. While it is valid, no turnover inspection is needed.

Does a lead-free certificate expire?

N.J.A.C. 5:28A says a lead-free certification issued at final clearance after abatement exempts the unit from future periodic inspections. Units certified lead-free under N.J.A.C. 5:17 are exempt from the rental inspection rule.

Who can issue a lead-free certification?

A certified lead evaluation firm, on a DCA form, after inspecting all painted surfaces.
