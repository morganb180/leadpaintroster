# Lead-based paint disclosure and the buyer's 10-day window

Last updated 2026-10-31. Checked against the official sources listed at the end of this page. General information, not legal advice. Rules change, so confirm details with the agency named.

Under the federal Lead-Based Paint Disclosure Rule, sellers and landlords of most housing built before 1978 must, before a contract or lease is signed, give the EPA pamphlet, disclose known lead paint and hazards, share any reports, and include a Lead Warning Statement. Buyers also get a 10-day period to have a lead inspection or risk assessment, which they can change or waive in writing.

At a glance

* Applies to most pre-1978 housing that is sold or leased ([EPA](https://www.epa.gov/lead/real-estate-disclosures-about-potential-lead-hazards)).
* Required before signing: the Protect Your Family From Lead in Your Home pamphlet, known information, available records, and a Lead Warning Statement.
* Buyers get 10 days to inspect. The parties can lengthen, shorten or waive it in writing.
* Renters get disclosure but no inspection period. Landlords are not required to test.
* Keep signed disclosures for 3 years.
* The rule requires disclosure only. It does not require testing or removal.

## Who must disclose, and when?

Sellers, landlords, real estate agents and property managers must give the disclosure before a buyer signs a sales contract or a renter signs a lease ([EPA](https://www.epa.gov/lead/real-estate-disclosures-about-potential-lead-hazards)). It covers most pre-1978 private housing, public housing, federally owned housing and housing receiving federal assistance.

Agents must tell sellers and landlords about their obligations and share responsibility for compliance, unless the seller or landlord failed to tell the agent what they knew.

## What must be disclosed

* **The EPA pamphlet** Protect Your Family From Lead in Your Home ([EPA](https://www.epa.gov/lead/protect-your-family-lead-your-home-real-estate-disclosure)), available in several languages.
* **Known information** about lead-based paint and hazards, such as where it is and its condition.
* **All available records and reports**, including common-area and building-wide reports in multi-unit buildings.
* **A Lead Warning Statement** in the contract or lease, or attached to it, confirming the seller or landlord complied. EPA publishes sample seller and lessor disclosure forms in English and Spanish.

Disclosure can be electronic if the buyer or renter consents and can access and keep the records ([EPA](https://www.epa.gov/lead/real-estate-disclosures-about-potential-lead-hazards)).

## How the buyer's 10-day window works

Sellers must give homebuyers a 10-day period to have a lead-based paint inspection or risk assessment ([EPA](https://www.epa.gov/lead/real-estate-disclosures-about-potential-lead-hazards)). Key points:

* The buyer and seller can agree in writing to lengthen or shorten it.
* The buyer can waive it.
* Use a certified inspector or risk assessor. EPA's Lead-Based Paint Professional Locator lists them.

Your purchase contract decides what happens if the inspection finds lead, so discuss contingency wording with your agent or attorney. ## Renters

Renters must get the same pamphlet, known information, records and warning statement before signing a lease. There is no inspection period for leases. Renters can ask the landlord to have a certified inspection done, but the rule does not require it ([EPA](https://www.epa.gov/lead/real-estate-disclosures-about-potential-lead-hazards)). Local laws may require more, as in NJ and NYC below.

## What is exempt?

The federal rule does not apply to ([EPA](https://www.epa.gov/lead/real-estate-disclosures-about-potential-lead-hazards)):

* Housing built after 1977.
* Zero-bedroom units such as efficiencies, lofts and dorms, unless a child under 6 lives or is expected to live there.
* Leases of 100 days or less with no renewal or extension.
* Housing for the elderly or people with disabilities, unless a child under 6 lives or is expected to live there.
* Housing tested by a certified inspector or risk assessor and found free of lead-based paint.
* Foreclosure sales.

## Records and penalties

Keep a signed copy of the disclosures for 3 years after the sale closes or the lease begins. Sellers, landlords, agents and managers who do not give proper disclosure may face penalties, and buyers or renters who did not get one can report it at epa.gov/lead/violation ([EPA](https://www.epa.gov/lead/real-estate-disclosures-about-potential-lead-hazards)).

## Extra rules in NJ, NYC and MA

* **Massachusetts:** sellers and agents must also give the state Property Transfer Lead Paint Notification before a purchase and sale agreement, share any compliance letters, and tell buyers that if a child under 6 will live there, the home must be deleaded or brought into Interim Control within 90 days of taking title ([Mass.gov](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/property-transfer-lead-paint-notification)). See the [Massachusetts Lead Law guide](https://leadpaintroster.com/massachusetts-lead-law/).
* **New Jersey:** sellers of rentals covered by the state lead inspection law must give the buyer the rules, any certifications and DCA's guidance document ([N.J.A.C. 5:28A-3.1](https://www.nj.gov/dca/codes/codreg/pdf_regs/njac_5_28A.pdf)). See [NJ's rental lead law](https://leadpaintroster.com/nj-lead-paint-inspection-law/).
* **New York City:** landlords of covered buildings certify turnover work on a Lease/Commencement of Occupancy Notice and give tenants HPD's pamphlet ([NYC HPD](https://www.nyc.gov/site/hpd/services-and-information/lead-based-paint-correction-child-under-6-unit.page)). See [NYC Local Law 1](https://leadpaintroster.com/nyc-local-law-1-lead-paint/).

## Frequently asked questions

**Does a seller have to test for lead paint before selling?**

No. The federal rule requires disclosure of what the seller knows and any records they have. It does not require testing or removal. The buyer can test during the 10-day window.

**Can the 10-day inspection period be waived?**

Yes. The buyer can waive it, and buyer and seller can agree in writing to lengthen or shorten it.

**Do landlords have to give a lead paint disclosure?**

Yes, for most pre-1978 rentals, before the lease is signed. Leases of 100 days or less with no renewal are exempt.

**How long must disclosure forms be kept?**

At least 3 years after the sale is completed or the lease begins.
