Older homes by county: how much housing predates lead paint bans

Last updated 2026-10-31. Checked against the official sources listed at the end of this page. Figures are U.S. Census Bureau survey estimates, not counts of homes with lead paint.

Most homes in the New York City area, northern New Jersey and much of Massachusetts were built before 1980. In Census data, Nassau County (85.4%), Queens County (82.6%), Chemung County (81.7%) have the highest shares in New York, Union County (78.3%), Passaic County (77.4%), Salem County (75.2%) in New Jersey, and Hampden County (76.3%), Berkshire County (75.9%), Suffolk County (72.1%) in Massachusetts. Homes built before 1978 may have lead-based paint.

At a glance

  • Source: U.S. Census Bureau, American Community Survey 2020-2024 5-year estimates, table B25034, Year Structure Built (Census).
  • Census groups years by decade, so the closest match to pre-1978 is built 1979 or earlier (before 1980).
  • Universe: all housing units, owned and rented, occupied and vacant.
  • Counts include a 90% margin of error. Shares are calculated by us from the Census counts.
  • Lead-based paint was banned for residential use in 1978 (EPA).

Why building age matters for lead

Homes built before 1978 may have lead-based paint, and EPA estimates about three-quarters of pre-1978 U.S. homes still contain some (EPA, EPA). Pre-1978 homes are also the ones covered by federal disclosure and RRP rules. NYC's Local Law 1 focuses on buildings built before 1960 (NYC HPD), so the before-1960 column matters there.

Age is only a clue. The only way to know whether a home has lead paint is to test it. See what is lead paint and inspection vs risk assessment.

How to read these tables

  • Housing units is the B25034 total for the county.
  • Built 1979 or earlier adds the Census categories 1970 to 1979, 1960 to 1969, 1950 to 1959, 1940 to 1949 and 1939 or earlier. It includes some homes built in 1978 and 1979, after the ban, because Census does not split the 1970s. The ± figure is a 90% margin of error we approximated from the Census category margins (square root of the sum of their squares).
  • Share before 1960 and before 1940 use the same totals (Census variable list).
  • Linked county names go to our list of certified lead pros in that county.

Frequently asked questions

How can I find out what year my home was built?

Check your deed, original title or municipal tax records. Renters can ask the landlord. Census data only shows county-level patterns, not individual buildings.

Why does this page use 1980 instead of 1978?

The Census Bureau's year-built table groups the 1970s into one category, so built 1979 or earlier is the closest available match to pre-1978.

Does an older home definitely have lead paint?

No. Older homes are more likely to have it, but only testing by a certified inspector or risk assessor can confirm it.

Where does this data come from?

U.S. Census Bureau American Community Survey 2020-2024 5-year estimates, table B25034 (Year Structure Built), downloaded from data.census.gov.
