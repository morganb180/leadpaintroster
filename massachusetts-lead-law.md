# Massachusetts Lead Law: deleading, compliance letters and tax credits

Last updated 2026-10-31. Checked against the official sources listed at the end of this page. General information, not legal advice. Rules change, so confirm details with the agency named.

The Massachusetts Lead Law requires lead paint hazards to be removed or covered in homes built before 1978 where a child under 6 lives. Owners comply by having a licensed inspector test the home, having the hazards deleaded, and getting a Letter of Full Deleading Compliance, or temporarily through Interim Control for up to 2 years.

At a glance

* Applies to homes built before 1978 where any child under 6 lives, including owner-occupied single-family homes ([Mass.gov](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/learn-about-massachusetts-lead-law)).
* Full compliance: licensed inspector, licensed deleader, reinspection, then a Letter of Full Deleading Compliance.
* Interim Control: fix urgent hazards first, get a Letter of Interim Control, then up to 2 years to fully comply ([Mass.gov](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/learn-about-interim-control-of-lead-paint-hazards)).
* Owners cannot evict or refuse to rent to families because of lead paint.
* State tax credit: up to $3,000 per unit for full compliance, up to $1,000 for interim control ([Mass. DOR](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/massachusetts-residential-property-tax-credits)).
* Questions: CLPPP at (800) 532-9571 or (617) 624-5757.

## What does the Lead Law require?

The law (M.G.L. c. 111 § 189A and regulations at 105 CMR 460) requires the removal or covering of lead paint hazards in homes built before 1978 where any child under 6 lives ([Mass.gov](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/learn-about-massachusetts-lead-law)). Lead paint hazards include loose lead paint, lead paint on windows and friction surfaces, and other surfaces children can reach.

Owners are responsible. That includes landlords and owners living in their own single-family home.

## How to comply: full deleading

1. Hire a licensed lead inspector to test the home and record all lead hazards.
2. Have the deleading work done by a trained and licensed person.
3. A licensed lead inspector reinspects. If the home passes, you receive a Letter of Full Deleading Compliance ([Mass.gov](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/learn-about-massachusetts-lead-law)).

Some work must be done by a licensed deleader. An owner or agent can do certain specific tasks, but only after a licensed inspector has inspected the home and the owner or agent has been trained and authorized ([Mass.gov](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/learn-about-massachusetts-lead-law)). Call CLPPP before starting.

## Interim Control: a temporary option

Instead of deleading everything at once, you may correct urgent lead hazards first and control the rest ([Mass.gov](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/learn-about-interim-control-of-lead-paint-hazards)):

* Hire a licensed risk assessor, who explains what work is needed.
* After the work, the risk assessor returns, checks the work and takes dust samples.
* If it passes, you receive a Letter of Interim Control. It is good for 1 year and can be renewed once, for a maximum of 2 years.
* Before it runs out, you must fully delead if a child under 6 lives there, even if the home has been sold.

Our [inspection vs risk assessment guide](https://leadpaintroster.com/lead-inspection-vs-risk-assessment/) explains the two roles.

## Tenants, discrimination and liability

Owners cannot evict or refuse to rent to anyone because of lead paint. Discrimination is against the law and carries penalties ([Mass.gov](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/learn-about-massachusetts-lead-law)). Owners of rental property are legally responsible if a child is poisoned by lead hazards where the child lives, and a tenant agreement accepting lead paint does not remove that liability. Complying with the Lead Law is the best protection.

## Selling or buying a pre-1978 home

Before signing a purchase and sale agreement (or a lease with option to buy, or a foreclosure memorandum), the seller or agent must give the buyer the Property Transfer Lead Paint Notification, any lead inspection, risk assessment or compliance letters, and must tell the buyer about the rule that a new owner has 90 days after taking title to delead or reach interim control if a child under 6 lives there ([Mass.gov](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/property-transfer-lead-paint-notification)). The buyer signs a certification. State law sets civil penalties, and federal law adds civil and criminal penalties. See our [disclosure guide](https://leadpaintroster.com/lead-paint-disclosure/) for the federal rules.

## Tax credits for deleading

The Massachusetts Department of Revenue lead paint credit is ([Mass. DOR](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/massachusetts-residential-property-tax-credits)):

* **Full compliance:** the lesser of your cost or $3,000 per unit.
* **Interim control:** the lesser of half your cost or $1,000 per unit. Interim control spending counts toward the $3,000 maximum.
* Unused credit can be carried forward for up to 7 years. Claim it on Schedule LP, with Schedule CMS.

These amounts took effect January 1, 2023 ([Mass.gov](https://www.mass.gov/news/increase-in-tax-credits-for-deleading)). Ask DOR at (617) 887-6367 about your situation.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I have to delead if no children under 6 live in my home?**

The Lead Law's deleading duty applies when a child under 6 lives in a pre-1978 home. You still must give notices when you sell, and you cannot refuse to rent to families because of lead paint.

**How long does a Letter of Interim Control last?**

One year, renewable once, for a maximum of two years. After that, the home must be fully deleaded if a child under 6 lives there.

**Can I delead my own home?**

Partly. An owner or agent can do some specific tasks after a licensed inspector has inspected and after they are trained and authorized. Other work must be done by a licensed deleader. Call CLPPP first.

**How much is the Massachusetts deleading tax credit?**

Up to $3,000 per unit for full compliance and up to $1,000 for interim control, per the Department of Revenue. Unused credit can carry forward up to 7 years.
